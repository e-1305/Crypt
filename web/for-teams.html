The problem

Security teams drown in dependency findings that nobody owns. In our census of 20 popular open-source projects (lockfiles only, not a random sample):

  • 248 findings across 11,244 dependencies.
  • 95.2% of those findings were noise (not actively exploited, low chance of exploitation), so 3 monsters remained.
  • 84.7% of dependencies had no CODEOWNERS owner, and 16 of 20 repositories have no CODEOWNERS file at all.

Source: docs/census/RESULTS.md, scan date 2026-11-17. Aggregates only, no project is named. Reproduce with npm run census.

A two-week read-only pilot

One organization, two weeks. CRYPT starts read-only: it reads, it ranks, it writes reports. It writes nothing to your repositories until you say so.

  1. Days 1 to 2. Run the Graveyard over the organization with a read-only token. You get one view of every repository, with the noise line applied.
  2. Days 3 to 5. Walk through the War Room and the CISO report on your own data. Decide which repositories get fix pull requests.
  3. Week 2. Enable the GitHub Action on the repositories you chose. CRYPT opens one pull request per fix. Your team reviews and merges.
  4. End of week 2. You decide: stop, or continue. Remove the token and nothing remains running.

Source: docs/ROADMAP.md, P5 and token scopes. No customer has completed this pilot yet.

Time saved ESTIMATE

hours saved per month = repos × findings per repo per month × noise share × triage minutes ÷ 60

An estimate, not a measurement. Change the inputs to match your team. Everything is computed in your browser and nothing is sent.

ESTIMATE: 200 findings, 190 filtered as noise, about 48 hours saved per month.

Defaults: 20 repositories, 10 findings per repo (median before filtering) and 95.2% noise are from the census. The census median is per scan, and we use it as a per-month figure here, which is an assumption. 15 triage minutes per finding is our assumption, not measured. Replace it with your own.
